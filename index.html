▸ Column legend — meaning & formula of every column
ColumnWhat it meansWhere the number comes from
Ref The row's number in the bill's outline. Given by the row's position and depth: 1 → 1.1 → 1.1.1. Renumbered on its own when rows are added, moved or deleted.
Work description The item of work, material or labour being priced. Typed by the user. A row with sub-rows is a heading and is never priced on its own — unless it also carries a quantity and a rate, which are then added to its subtotal.
Brand The product make or brand used for the item. Typed by the user. Carried into the CSV and Excel exports.
Unit The unit the quantity is measured in — m³, m², m, nr, kg … Chosen from the Unit drop-down. The stored value is the symbol (m3, m2, nr …); the drop-down shows the full name.
Quantity (Drawing) The quantity measured off the drawing. Typed by the user, or brought in with an imported bill.
Quantity Mark-up % Mark-up % added on top of the drawing quantity. Typed per row. The reference sheets use 10%. Blank or 0 means the drawing quantity is charged as measured.
Charged Quantity The quantity actually charged for: the drawing quantity plus its mark-up, always rounded up. ROUNDUP(Quantity × (1 + Quantity Mark-up % ÷ 100))Rounded up to 2 decimals. 0 when the quantity is 0.
Material Price Optional fixed material unit price for this one row. Typed per row. Used only when no Original Rate — Material is entered; otherwise the Material rate list price applies.
Labour Price Optional fixed labour unit price for this one row. Typed per row. Used only when no Original Rate — Labour is entered; otherwise the Labour rate list price applies.
Original Rate — Material The base material unit rate, before any mark-up. Typed per row. When it is blank the row falls back to Material Price, then to the Material rate list.
Original Rate — Labour The base labour unit rate, before any mark-up. Typed per row. When it is blank the row falls back to Labour Price, then to the Labour rate list.
Mark-up % — Material The mark-up % applied on top of the original material rate. Typed per row.
Mark-up % — Labour The mark-up % applied on top of the original labour rate. Typed per row.
Rate — Material The material unit rate actually used to price the row. ROUNDUP(Original Rate — Material × (1 + Mark-up % — Material ÷ 100))Rounded up to 2 decimals, the way the Excel sheets do it. With no Original Rate the row keeps its Material Price / rate-list price instead.
Rate — Labour The labour unit rate actually used to price the row. ROUNDUP(Original Rate — Labour × (1 + Mark-up % — Labour ÷ 100))Rounded up to 2 decimals, the way the Excel sheets do it. With no Original Rate the row keeps its Labour Price / rate-list price instead.
Total — Material The material cost of the line. Total — Material = Rate — Material × Charged Quantity
Total — Labour The labour cost of the line. Total — Labour = Rate — Labour × Charged Quantity
Amount What the line is worth to the bill. Amount = Total — Material + Total — Labour
Budget What the line costs at the original rates — the cost side, before any mark-up. Budget = (Original Rate — Material + Original Rate — Labour) × Charged QuantityFalls back to the Material / Labour Price or rate-list price when an Original Rate is blank.
Profit The mark-up earned on the line. Profit = Amount - BudgetThis is the difference between the marked-up rates and the original ones.
Remark A free-text note about the row. Typed by the user. Carried into the CSV and Excel exports.
Prices, rates and totals are worked out on every keystroke, so you never re-enter a figure by hand. Every money figure is shown rounded to 2 decimals with a $ sign — that rounding is for display only. On a heading row (a row with sub-items) the price, mark-up, rate and total columns show —, while Amount, Budget and Profit become the total of everything beneath that heading. The footer repeats those same three figures across the whole bill.
ⓘ The bill is arranged as a hierarchy: 1 → 1.1 → 1.1.1. Headings roll up the totals of everything under them; only item rows take a unit, a quantity and rates. Click a row to select it, then use the big buttons above the list.
